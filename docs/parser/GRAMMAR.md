# ECMA-262's grammars as Lean data

Pinned ECMA-262 726ec8a4. The parser that reads this data: [README.md](README.md).

The grammars of spec.html are Lean data in the component `Ecma.Grammar` (`lean/Ecma/Grammar/`, namespace `Grammar`), generated by the Rust crate `crates/jsgrammar` and checked by a round trip back to spec.html's text. The crate is a library (`gl`: the grammars with one numbering of their nonterminals and right-hand sides; `keys`: the resolver of the spec's other `<emu-grammar>` blocks to those ids) and the generator binary; neither is trusted. What is trusted of the data is "it prints as spec.html's grammar", what the notation means (§5.1.6), which `Core.lean` writes as types, and the printer's templates (`Print.lean`), among them the fragments of prose each fact of the index rests on ("The index", below). The index (`tests/parser/grammar_index.txt`) is a reviewed record that the round trip compares line for line, so any change to it is seen.

## The component

`Ecma.Grammar` is a component of its own (`build_defs/architecture.bzl`): in `JS_DEPS`, and in `ISOLATED` (rule 11) with no component it may import. `Engine.Parse` (the parser) may import only it. The specification's model (`Ecma.Model`) imports `Ecma.Grammar.Core`, the notation's types, and nothing of the generated data. The base modules derive only `Repr`, `BEq` and `Inhabited` (no `DecidableEq`).

## Regenerating

```
python3 scripts/jsgrammar.py --write ...   # writes lean/Ecma/Grammar/Gen from the pinned spec.html (README.md)
git diff                                   # a no-op on an unchanged pin (`--check-regen` checks it)
```

`//:jsgrammar` is `scripts/jsgrammar.py --write`, which runs the generator `//:jsgrammar-gen` (the crate's binary; `--prods` prints the numbering, `--keys` every key block's resolution) on `@ecma262`'s spec.html, the submodule `external/ecma262` at its pin (`build_defs/submodules.bzl`). The generator deletes the directory's other `.lean` files. After a pin move, regenerate, then fix what the tests report: a new form of the notation is an error of the generator until `Core.lean`, the generator and the printer know it; a new lint finding is a generator bug or an errata candidate.

Every generated module is constructor and `List` data (no `#[…]`, no computed table) and elaborates in under 2.5 s (the target is 10 s, a convention measured at each regeneration and checked at review; Bazel enforces only `budgets.bzl`'s 30 s; the slowest are the 471- and 442-constructor `Production` types). The data is about 8,900 lines.

The grammars are split by colons and clause, as §5.1 has them: the **numeric string grammar** (`:::`: StringNumericLiteral, StringIntegerLiteral, UTCOffset), the **lexical grammar** (`::` outside §22.2.1), the **syntactic grammar** (`:`, with its supplemental grammars and NativeFunction), the **RegExp grammar** (`::` of §22.2.1, `sec-patterns`), and **Annex B**'s overlays.

## Production ids

GL numbers the right-hand sides of all grammars (`ProdId`, a `Nat`; `Grammar.prods`): grammar by grammar (numeric string, lexical, syntactic, RegExp, Annex B, JSON), then by written production in each `Production` type's constructor order, then over the presences of its optional symbols. An alternative with `k` optional symbols "actually specifies" `2^k` right-hand sides (§5.1.6, spec.html:724), one id each, in mask order: absent before present, the first optional symbol the most significant. A `one of` terminal is one right-hand side. A right-hand side's `ProdId` is its alphabetical index among all expanded right-hand side names (`Gen.prodIds`, `GL.ids`; `Grammar.prods` is by id), and `NtId` is `NT`'s constructor order (alphabetically sorted nonterminal names). A Parse Node's production is its expanded id.

The ids are frozen as the index's `prod ID GRAMMAR MASK NAME` lines (`MASK` one `0`/`1` per optional symbol, `-` for none): the round trip compares Lean's numbering with them, and `//:jsgrammar_regen_test` the generator's. A renumbering is a reviewed diff of those lines.

## Keys

A key is a production named outside a definition: an SDO row's or an early-error rule's `<emu-grammar>`, one in a step or in prose. §5.2.2 writes it "without any “[ ]” grammar annotations", and an optional symbol in it, `X?`, stands for both of its presences. The resolver (`crates/jsgrammar`'s `keys`, `Gl::resolve`) gives each key block the ids of the right-hand sides of its left-hand side whose symbols are one of the key's expansions, every expansion naming at least one; so `ArrayBindingPattern : [ Elision? ]` is 2 of the 4 right-hand sides of `[ Elision? BindingRestElement? ]`, and `Script : [empty]` the empty one of `Script : ScriptBody?`. The grammar is the one the colons and the clause give: inside an Annex B section, that overlay's definition of the left-hand side if it has one; else the main grammar's, `::` in the RegExp chapter or B.1.2 being RegExp's where the lexical grammar also defines the name. A main grammar key also names the right-hand sides of the same symbols in an overlay of that grammar that redefines its left-hand side: B.1.1 extends "the syntax and semantics" of §12.4, and B.1.2 modifies §22.2.1's syntax while each of its semantic sub-clauses says the main semantics "is extended as follows", so the main rules hold at the productions an overlay keeps. `AtomEscape :: DecimalEscape` in §22.2 names RegExp's 1129 and B.1.2's 1364 and 1365. This adds 37 Annex B ids to 29 key blocks of §22.2. A key inside an overlay's section names that overlay's definition of its left-hand side when it has one, else the main grammar's.

All 1,418 key blocks of the pin resolve (`Gen.keys`). The round trip checks each: its text is spec.html's, verbatim, and `keyText` of its ids is `stripKey` of its text (one `Lhs : rhs` line per production, annotations and labels dropped, `X?` as written). `crates/spec-gen` resolves its SDO rows' and early-error rules' keys with the same library.

## The notation (§5.1.6) and its constructors

| spec.html | `Core.lean` |
|---|---|
| `X :`, `X ::`, `X :::` | `Rule.colons`: `.one`, `.two`, `.three` |
| `X[In, Yield] :` | `Rule.params` |
| `X :: one of` and its rows | `Body.oneOf rows` (each terminal is a production) |
| `` `if` ``, ``` ``` ``` (the backtick), `` `&grave;&grave;` `` | `Terminal.text` (character references decoded) |
| `<LF>` (and the other names of Tables 34–35 spec.html uses) | `Terminal.cp .LF` |
| `Expression[+In, ~Yield, ?Await]` | `Sym.nt .Expression [⟨.plus, .In⟩, ⟨.tilde, .Yield⟩, ⟨.quest, .Await⟩]` |
| `X?` (opt) | `Item.sym s true` |
| `[+In]`, `[+Using, +Await]` before an alternative | `Alt.guard` (every condition must hold) |
| `[lookahead == s]`, `[lookahead != s]` | `Lookahead.eq`, `.ne` |
| `[lookahead ∈ { s, t }]`, `[lookahead ∉ { s, t }]` | `Lookahead.mem`, `.notMem` |
| `[lookahead ∉ DecimalDigit]` | `Lookahead.notMemNT` |
| `[no LineTerminator here]` (also inside a lookahead sequence) | `Item.noLT`, `LookItem.noLT` |
| `[empty]` | `Item.empty` |
| `X but not Y`, `X but not one of A or B`, `X but not one of A B` | `Item.butNot X (.sym Y)`, `(.oneOf [A, B] true)`, `(.oneOf [A, B] false)` |
| `[> but only if …]` | `Item.onlyIf` with a `Phrase` (6) |
| `> any Unicode code point …` | `Item.phrase` with a `Phrase` (5) |
| `#parencover` | `Alt.label` |
| a block after "the interpretation of \|C\| is refined using the following grammar" | `BlockKind.supplemental C`, and a `Cover` |
| a block of Annex B | `BlockKind.overlay section mode cond`: the mode `extend` (B.1.1 "is extended as follows", B.3.3 and B.3.5 "augments"; the union with the main definition, a restated alternative counted once) or `replace` (B.1.2 "is modified and extended as follows", in order); the condition `notModule` (B.1.1 "except that this extension is not allowed when parsing source text using the goal symbol \|Module\|"), `nonStrict` (B.3.3 and B.3.5 "This production only applies when parsing non-strict code") or `always` (B.1.2) |

The generator fails on anything else: an unknown form, phrase, code point name or character reference, trailing white space, an alternative not indented by two spaces, a block mixing grammars, a duplicate definition within a grammar or a duplicate production, an Annex B section or prose it does not know (a section whose mode phrase or condition sentence changed, or whose prose states a condition it does not know: "only applies", "not allowed", "only when", "strict", \|Script\| or \|Module\| outside a known sentence), an `<emu-prodref>` (Annex A, 388) naming no definition or label, a supplemental grammar whose production it cannot find, or a supplemental grammar given as a copy (AwaitExpression's, §13.5) that differs from the definition.

## Canonical counts

The conventions: a **definition** is one `Name … :` heading of a block (a nonterminal defined in the main grammar and in an overlay counts twice); a **production** is one alternative of a definition, `opt` not expanded, a `one of` definition's terminals one each; an alternative's guard is part of it. The counts are the report's `@@count` lines, from the data, and the checked record is the `count` lines of `tests/parser/grammar_index.txt` (per grammar: blocks, definitions, productions; the notation's forms; goals, covers, semicolons by class). At pin 726ec8a4 they are 90 blocks (+ 32 examples, not read), 411 definitions, 1,250 productions (numeric string 50, lexical 442, syntactic with its supplemental grammars 471, RegExp 198, Annex B 89), 399 nonterminals and 13 parameters.

The grammars' parameters, in first-declaration order: Sep, Yield, Await, In, Tagged, Return, Default, Pattern, Using, Extended, UnicodeMode, UnicodeSetsMode, NamedCaptureGroups.

**Covers** (`Gen.covers`) are the supplemental-grammar covers only: the sites whose prose says "the interpretation of |C| is refined using the following grammar", 6 supplemental blocks (ParenthesizedExpression, CallMemberExpression, AssignmentPattern and its 10 helpers, AwaitUsingDeclarationHead, ArrowFormalParameters, AsyncArrowHead) and one copy of a definition (AwaitExpression, spec.html:20552), each with the production whose instance it refines (the index's `cover` lines). The early errors' "must cover" sites (DestructuringAssignmentTarget : LeftHandSideExpression, spec.html:21512; the LeftHandSideExpression of the for-in, for-of and for-await-of heads, spec.html:22780) are not in it: they are rows the machine runs over the parser's trees.

The goal symbols (`Gen.goals`) are those spec.html names: the grammars' goal symbols of §5.1 (the five InputElement goals, Pattern, StringNumericLiteral, Script, Module), ParseText's goals (StringIntegerLiteral, StrDecimalLiteral, `HexDigits[~Sep]`, UTCOffset, the four Pattern instances), CreateDynamicFunction's grammar symbols (the expression, body and `FormalParameters[±Yield, ±Await]` goals of its kind table), NativeFunction ("must have the syntax of"), and the targets of "must cover" and of the supplemental grammars.

**§12.10's semicolons** (`Gen.semis`, by hard-coded rules of the generator, so reviewed in the index): rule 1's do-while case is DoWhileStatement's `;` (`doWhile`: insertable by rules 1–3 as any `insertable` one, and also after `)` by the special case); "parsed as an empty statement" is EmptyStatement's; "one of the two semicolons in the header of a for statement" is ForStatement's own (5), and also the `;` that ends the LexicalDeclaration of `for ( LexicalDeclaration Expression? ; …` (LexicalDeclaration's, UsingDeclaration's and AwaitUsingDeclaration's: the right ends of LexicalDeclaration, computed by the generator), which is insertable elsewhere: `forHeaderIfDeclaration`, decided by context in the parser (`Parse/Syntax.lean`).

## The index

The generator also computes what no grammar text shows: which prose a block follows (its kind; for Annex B its mode and condition), the goal symbols (scraped from prose), the covers, §12.10's class of each `;`, the alternatives an `extend` overlay restates, the counts and the production ids. The report prints them (`@@count`, `@@kind`, `@@goal`, `@@cover`, `@@semi`, `@@restated`, `@@prod`), and `//:jsgrammar_roundtrip_test` requires them to be the lines of `tests/parser/grammar_index.txt`, in order; after a pin move or a generator change the test lists each line that changed, for review.

The index's facts are not trusted output of the generator. Each prints the fragments of spec.html's raw text that state it (`Cite`, `Print.lean`), and the round trip finds each where it says:

- an overlay's mode and condition: its section's phrase (B.1.1 "is extended as follows", B.1.2 "is modified and extended as follows", B.3.3 and B.3.5 "The following augments the |X| production") and condition sentence, in the section's own prose, and the block is in that section;
- a supplemental grammar and a cover: "the interpretation of |C| is refined using the following grammar:" before the block; the refined grammar is the block's first definition (or the copy's key, AwaitExpression's at spec.html:20552), and the key before the sentence names the cover's production;
- a goal: the words that name it on its line ("starting from" … |X|, "ParseText(_" … ", |X|)", "be the grammar symbol |X|", "must have the syntax of a |X|", "must cover a" … |X|), or the supplemental block or copy it is the goal of;
- §12.10's classes: `Lint.semis` recomputes them from the grammar by the rules its sentences state, which are cited in §12.10's prose;
- the counts, the restated alternatives and the ids are computed in Lean.

What stays trusted is that the citation templates read the prose rightly, and that the lists are complete (a block whose prose stated a kind the generator does not know would be `main`; a goal the generator's scan misses would be absent): the generator fails on any Annex B prose that states a condition it does not know.

The restated alternatives are B.1.1's: its InputElementHashbangOrRegExp repeats the main definition's six alternatives (and adds HTMLCloseComment), its Comment both of the main ones. Under `extend`'s union each is one production, not two. B.1.1's MultiLineComment is different: it is a new alternative (`/*` FirstCommentLine? LineTerminator MultiLineCommentChars? `*/` HTMLCloseComment?), not a restatement, and a comment such as `/*a⏎b*/` derives by both it and the main MultiLineComment; that is a lexical ambiguity of B.1.1 itself, for the lexer to treat as one token.

## Round trip

`scripts/bijection.py` (README.md, "The bijection") runs `ecma-print` (`lean/SpecPrint.lean`): `Block.printExact` prints every block of `Gen.blocks`, and the check requires it to be spec.html's text between the block's tags (`<emu-grammar type="definition">`, not the examples), found by the check itself, **byte for byte**, each block once and in order, json.emu's after spec.html's; and each rule reading (`Gen.Reading`) to be the overlay's definition, its hash that of the pinned rule's text in its clause. The surface of the text is data the derivation relation ignores:

- a terminal's spelling: spec.html writes `<`, `&`, the backtick and non-ASCII characters by reference, and `>` too, but in three `=>` (`Terminal.text s true`); the lone backtick is ``` ``` ```, but once `` `&grave;` `` (37467, `true` as well). Conditions keep their `>` raw and descriptive phrases start `&gt;`, always;
- a block's indentation (`Block.indent`) and the rules a `// emu-format ignore` line precedes (`Block.fmtIgnore`).

The layout is otherwise fixed (a newline, one blank line between rules, alternatives two spaces in, the closing tag two spaces out), and the generator fails on a block laid out otherwise, or a spelling it does not know.

Not run today: the report that also held the key blocks' resolutions, the citations, the index, the lint findings and the `Production` types' names (`scripts/jsgrammar_query.lean`, with `Lint.lean`, `Prods.lean` and `Cite`) was removed with the code only it reached, so the checks of the sections "Keys" (the key text against its ids), "The index" (beyond its `prod` lines, which `--check-regen` compares), "Lints" and "Residue", and the check that the i-th constructor of a `Production` type is the i-th alternative, have no runner; the key blocks of SDO rows and early-error rules are compared verbatim by `scripts/bijection.py`.

## Lints

`Lint.lean` runs over `Gen.blocks` and `Gen.goals` in the report; the round-trip test requires its findings to be exactly the accepted ones of `tests/parser/grammar_lints.txt` (a new finding fails, and so does an accepted one no longer found).

| Lint | What it checks | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| `undefined` | every nonterminal a right-hand side, a `but not`, a `lookahead ∉ N` or a goal names has a definition | none |
| `params` | a guard and a `?P` argument name a parameter of their definition; an argument names a parameter of the nonterminal it is passed to (in a B.1.2 overlay, of the overlay's own definition if it has one), at most once; every declared parameter is used (by a guard or a `?P`); an `extend` overlay declares the parameters of the definition it extends; a goal's arguments are its nonterminal's parameters | 3, accepted: BindingIdentifier's Yield and Await, TemplateLiteral's Tagged, which no alternative reads but their early errors do (§13.1.1, §13.2.8.1) |
| `unreachable` | every defined nonterminal is reachable from a goal symbol (through every definition, overlays included) | none (AwaitExpression is reached through its cover, NativeFunction as a goal) |
| `unproductive` | every nonterminal derives some terminal string (parameters and guards ignored) | none |
| `lookahead` | no lookahead of the syntactic grammar starts with a token whose lexing depends on the lexical goal (`/`, `/=`, `}`, `#!`, `-->`; §5.1.6's "editorial error") | none |

The generator itself checks the rest of what the proposal listed: Annex A's 388 `<emu-prodref>`s resolve, labels included; every definition block of spec.html is read; the Annex B sections' prose says what their mode and condition assume.

## Grammar rule readings

Rules whose prose adds an alternative or lookahead restriction that `spec.html`'s `<emu-grammar>` block does not carry are written directly in `spec/spectec/04-grammar-regexp.spectec` (with `spec/html/grammar-meta.txt` recording the exact `spec.html` rule override for `spectec-html`), and their productions receive alphabetically sorted `ProdId`s alongside the rest of `GL`: `IdentityEscape` includes the two `[~UnicodeMode]` join-control alternatives U+200C (`&#x200C;`) and U+200D (`&#x200D;`), and `RegExpUnicodeEscapeSequence` carries the lookahead restriction on a lone `[+UnicodeMode] u HexLeadSurrogate` not followed by an escaped trail surrogate (spec.html 37311). Annex B's B.1.2 `replace` of `IdentityEscape` still applies when the Annex B overlay is in effect.

## Residue

What of the grammars is not data whose meaning the notation gives is listed in `tests/parser/grammar_residue.txt`, by the channel that carries it (ARC §3.4); the round trip requires its (b) rows to be exactly the data's declared primitives and each (c) row's sentence to be in its clause. Nothing is a stub.

- **(b) declared primitives**, each a primitive the derivation relation (`Derives`) declares and the base never defines:
  - the 5 descriptive phrases (`> any Unicode code point …`): code point classes, by the Unicode property or interval their text names;
  - `<USP>`: "any code point in general category “Space_Separator”" (§12.2's table, spec.html:17014; U+0020 and U+00A0 are in it, 17025). The other 8 names stand for one code point each, `CodePointName.codePoint`, cited by its table row, so they are data;
  - the 6 `[> but only if …]` conditions. Each reads a static semantics of the item before it (the MV of |HexDigits| or |Hex4Digits|; CapturingGroupNumber of |DecimalEscape| against CountLeftCapturingParensWithin of the containing |Pattern|). `Derives` reaches them as a parameter (`CodePrims.cond`): a predicate on the Parse Node derived for that item, given the whole input. The MV conditions are the instance's, by those SDOs' meaning over the node. The last is a cited Lean definition beside `Derives`, `Ecma.Grammar.groupCond`, which `ParsesCode` installs for B.1.2's grammar: its "the |Pattern| containing |DecimalEscape|" is the tree being chosen, so it is read stratified (spec decision `annexb-group-number-stratified`): CapturingGroupNumber (`decimalMV` of the |DecimalEscape|'s code points) at most CountLeftCapturingParensWithin (`GL.capturingIds`' nodes, |Atom|'s and B.1.2's |ExtendedAtom|'s) of the committed parse of the input as Pattern[~UnicodeMode, ~UnicodeSetsMode, ~NamedCaptureGroups] with the condition true. So `Derives` stays a §5.1 definition and the coupling to SDOs is the instantiation, never an assumption.
- **(c) annotated prose**, definitions over `Derives` that cite their sentence: the tokens of the syntactic grammar (§5.1.3); the lexer's longest match and the choice of lexical goal ("is permitted", §12); a |MultiLineComment| with a line terminator, and B.1.1's |SingleLineHTMLCloseComment|, as a |LineTerminator|; Hashbang Comments discarded; the |NumericLiteral| follow rule (§12.9.3); §12.10's three rules and the overriding condition ("not allowed by any production" is a definition over `Derives`); B.1.2's ordered alternatives (`Ecma.Grammar.Committed`, commitment: a node of a nonterminal B.1.2 restates, `GL.orderedNts`, uses an alternative only if no earlier one derives any span from its start, `Tree.Commits`: spec decision `annexb-regexp-order`; `ParsesCode` takes a committed parse where §B.1.2's overlay is in effect, `GL.ordered`); §11.2.2's strict mode code, which B.3.3's and B.3.5's "non-strict code" reads (`StrictCode`, `lean/Ecma/Grammar/StrictCode.lean`). The data keeps only what is mechanical: the `[no LineTerminator here]` positions and §12.10's class of each `;`.
- **(a) upstream:** no grammar defect found. The spelling inconsistencies (`=>` raw in three terminals, `=&gt;` in one; the lone backtick written `` `&grave;` `` once) are surface, carried as data.

The early-error rules, "must cover" and Annex B's early-error edits are `crates/spec-gen`'s rows (104 key blocks of Early Errors clauses, 219 rules, 140 keyed productions), keyed through the resolver.

## The notation's meaning

`Derives` is the grammar's derivation relation (`Ecma.Grammar`, `lean/Ecma/Grammar/Derives.lean`).
The descriptive phrases, `<USP>` and the `[> but only if …]` conditions are
its alphabet's primitives (`Alphabet.phrase`, `Alphabet.cond`): the data only
names them, and each instance gives their classes.
